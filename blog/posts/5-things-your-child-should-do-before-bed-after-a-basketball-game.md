The game finishes. Your child gets in the car tired, hungry and still thinking about the shot they missed in the fourth quarter. By the time you get home, it’s tempting to grab some food, jump on the phone and go straight to bed. 

But what happens in those few hours after a basketball game can make a difference to how a young athlete feels the next day. They don’t need ice baths, expensive recovery boots or the routine of a professional athlete. For most kids, recovery is much simpler. Here are five things young basketball players should do before bed after a game.

## **1. Eat Something**

After basketball, kids need fuel. They’ve been running, jumping, defending, changing direction and competing, sometimes for well over an hour when warm-ups are included. Their body has used energy. Now it needs some back.

A normal post-game meal containing carbohydrates and protein is a good place to start. That could be something as simple as:

- Chicken and rice
- Pasta with a protein source
- Eggs on toast
- A sandwich and fruit
- Yoghurt with fruit and cereal

It doesn’t need to be complicated. And it definitely doesn’t need to look like something an NBA nutritionist prepared. The bigger problem is often kids finishing a late game, saying they’re tired and going to bed without eating properly. Food is part of recovery.

## **2. Drink Water**

This sounds ridiculously obvious. But watch what happens after a junior basketball game. Kids finish playing. They talk to their teammates. They get in the car. They grab their phone. And the half-full water bottle they brought to the stadium stays half-full.

Basketball can be demanding, especially inside hot stadiums. Encourage your child to keep drinking water after the game rather than treating hydration as something that ends at the final buzzer. They don’t need to force huge amounts down at once. Just keep drinking normally throughout the evening.

## **3. Give Their Body a Chance to Settle Down**

A basketball game is chaotic. Sprint. Stop. Jump. Land. Change direction. Repeat. Your child’s body doesn’t immediately switch from competition mode to sleep mode because the game ended. A little gentle movement after the game can help them wind down.

That might mean walking for a few minutes, doing some comfortable mobility or simply moving around rather than immediately collapsing into the car and staying there. We’re not talking about another workout. The game was the workout. The goal now is recovery. And this is where training volume matters too.

Kids need enough basketball to improve, but they also need enough recovery to adapt to that training. More isn’t always better.

## **4. Don’t Turn the Car Ride Home Into a Performance Review**

This one isn’t physical. It might be even more important. Your child already knows they missed shots. They know they turned the ball over. They know whether they played well. And there’s a good chance they’re replaying parts of the game in their own head already.

The drive home doesn’t need to become:

_“Why didn’t you shoot that?”_

_“You weren’t aggressive enough.”_

_“What happened in the second half?”_

_“You need to stop making that mistake.”_

There’ll be time to learn from the game.

Immediately afterwards isn’t always that time.

Sometimes the best thing a parent can say is: **“I loved watching you play.”**

Let the emotional temperature come down. Let them talk if they want to talk. If they don’t, that’s okay too.

Young athletes need to learn how to process performances without believing every mistake requires an immediate investigation.

That’s especially important for kids who already hesitate because they’re worried about getting things wrong.

## **5. Get to Sleep**

Here’s the boring one. It’s also probably the most important. Sleep is when the body gets a proper opportunity to recover. And young athletes need it.

Yet late basketball games can make sleep tricky. You finish at 8:30pm. Drive home. Eat. Shower. Then somehow your child is watching YouTube at 10:47pm like they don’t have school tomorrow. We’ve all seen it.

You don’t need to create some military bedtime routine. Just make sleep a priority. Keep the post-game routine simple. Eat. Hydrate. Shower. Wind down. Phone away. Sleep.

A child who’s consistently training but consistently exhausted isn’t getting the full benefit of that training.

## **What About Stretching, Ice Baths and Recovery Tools?**

They can have their place. But parents shouldn’t mistake complicated recovery for good recovery. Young athletes don’t necessarily need:

❌ Expensive recovery boots
❌ A 30-minute stretching routine
❌ Ice baths after every game
❌ Five different supplements
❌ A professional athlete’s recovery protocol

Start with the basics. Food. Water. Sleep. Reasonable training loads. Good habits. Do those consistently before worrying about the fancy stuff.

## **What If My Child Has Another Game Tomorrow?**

Tournament weekends are where recovery becomes even more important. If your child is playing again the next morning, keep the evening boring. That’s a compliment. Eat properly. Hydrate. Get organised for tomorrow. Get to bed.

You don’t need to squeeze another shooting session in because they went 2-for-9. They’ve already played. Give their body a chance to recover. And remember that improving as a basketball player isn’t about winning every individual day. It’s about what happens across months and years.

[**`How Do Kids Become Better Shooters?`**](https://proball.com/blog/how-do-kids-become-better-shooters.html)

The same principle applies to shooting. One massive session rarely changes a player. Good repetitions accumulated consistently do.

## **Recovery Is Part of Training**

Young players often think improvement happens when they’re sweating. That’s only half the story. Training gives the body a reason to adapt. Recovery gives it the opportunity. And the better habits kids develop while they’re young, the easier it becomes to look after themselves when basketball gets more demanding later. So after the next game, don’t overcomplicate it.

### **The 5-Step Post-Game Routine**

**1. Eat something.**
Refuel after the game.

**2. Drink water.**
Keep hydrating throughout the evening.

**3. Wind down.**
Give the body time to settle.

**4. Leave the coaching at the stadium.**
Let your child decompress.

**5. Sleep.**
Give their body the recovery it actually needs.

That’s it. No $5,000 recovery room required. Just good habits repeated often.
