There are weekends in junior basketball where you remember the results. Then there are weekends where you remember everything else. The nervous faces before tip-off. The teammates screaming from the bench. The kid who makes a play they probably wouldn’t have attempted three months ago. The parents trying desperately not to look more nervous than their children. The celebrations. The disappointment. The hugs afterwards. This weekend had all of it.

It was **semi-finals weekend for ProBall**, and across the courts we got to watch our players experience exactly why we love junior basketball so much. Some of our teams are now heading to **Grand Finals**. For others, their season finished when the final buzzer sounded. But we’re proud of every single one of them.

## **Semi-Finals Have a Different Feeling**

You can feel it before the game even starts. Kids know something is different. There’s a little more nervous energy. The warm-up suddenly feels more serious. Every basket seems bigger. Every mistake feels bigger too. That’s part of learning to compete.

Young players need opportunities to experience those moments. Not because winning a junior basketball semi-final is the most important thing in the world. It isn’t.

Learning how to perform when you’re nervous, how to respond when something goes wrong and how to keep competing when a game gets difficult are experiences that stay with young athletes, and this weekend gave our players plenty of them.

## **Some Teams Are Going to Grand Finals**

This was so exciting. For those teams, there’s another game to prepare for. Another week of training. Another opportunity to compete together. They’ve earned the chance to play for a championship, and we can’t wait to watch them have a crack at it.

There’s something we want our players to understand. **Making a Grand Final isn’t the only evidence that you’ve improved.** Sometimes the biggest progress happens on teams whose seasons have already finished.

## **For the Teams Whose Seasons Ended This Weekend**

This bit matters. Losing a semi-final hurts. Especially when you’re young. You don’t necessarily want to hear about “development” five minutes after losing a basketball game you desperately wanted to win. You wanted another game. That’s okay.

After the disappointment settles, we hope our players look back at where they started. Think about the first training session. The first game. The mistakes that used to happen every week. The things that felt difficult.

Now compare that player with the one who walked off the court this weekend. That’s the real scoreboard. Maybe you’re more confident with the ball. Maybe you’re defending harder. Maybe you’re starting to understand spacing. Maybe you’re communicating with teammates. Maybe you’ve learned how to respond after making a mistake instead of letting it ruin the next five possessions. Maybe you’ve simply fallen a little more in love with basketball. Those things matter.

## **This Is What Junior Basketball Should Look Like**

Of course we want our teams to win. Anyone telling kids to compete while pretending the result doesn’t matter at all probably hasn’t spent much time around competitive kids. **They want to win.** 

That’s a good thing. We want them to compete hard. We want them chasing loose balls. We want them trying to make the right play when the game gets tight. But the result can’t become the only measure of whether the experience was worthwhile.

Junior basketball should teach kids how to: **Compete hard on every possession. Learn from wins and losses. Support teammates through good games and bad ones. Handle pressure and disappointment. Keep improving**, and, somewhere amongst all of that… **actually enjoy playing basketball.** Because they’re kids. That part matters too.

## **The Moments We’ll Remember**

Years from now, most of these kids won’t remember the exact score of their semi-final. They probably won’t remember where they finished on the ladder either. But they’ll remember their teammates.

They’ll remember the games that went down to the final possession. They’ll remember hitting a big shot. They’ll remember the teammate who picked them up after a mistake. They’ll remember celebrating together. They’ll remember being part of something. That’s one of the best things sport can give a kid.

## **Now We Keep Building**

For the teams heading into Grand Finals: **Enjoy the week. Prepare well. Then go compete.** For the teams whose seasons finished: **Be proud of how far you’ve come. Then get back to work.**

To every player, coach, parent and family who was part of the weekend, thank you.

Junior basketball doesn’t happen without people giving up their mornings, coaching teams, driving kids across Sydney, cheering from the sidelines and supporting young players through all the ups and downs that come with sport.

One weekend finishes. Another training starts. And we keep building.

**Compete. Learn. Have fun. Come back better.**

That’s ProBall. 💙🏀
