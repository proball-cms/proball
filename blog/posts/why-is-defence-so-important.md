Lots of kids fall in love with basketball because of scoring. They imagine hitting the game winner. Making three-pointers. Crossing someone over... They rarely dream about sliding their feet, taking a charge or forcing a turnover.

But there's also something almost every experienced coach knows: the quickest way to earn more minutes isn’t always becoming a better scorer. It’s becoming a player your coach trusts on defence.

That’s because every player has nights when the shots don’t fall. Defence doesn’t disappear. Effort doesn’t disappear. Communication doesn’t disappear. Those things travel to every game.

## **Defence Gets You On The Court**

Think about two players. One scores 12 points but gives up 18. The other scores six points but shuts down the other team’s best player. Which one helps the team win more?

Most coaches already know the answer. The second player becomes incredibly valuable. That’s because great defence helps everyone else play better too. It creates fast breaks. It forces bad shots. It changes momentum. It gives your teammates confidence.

Scoring is exciting. Defence wins possessions. And possessions win games.

## **Defence Is Mostly About Habits**

People often think great defenders are simply faster. Sometimes that’s true. But more often they’re just better prepared. They sprint back every time. They stay low. They talk. They watch both the ball and their player. They don’t give up after one mistake.

Those habits are built long before game day. They’re built during training. Every closeout. Every defensive slide. Every one on one drill. Eventually those habits become automatic.

## **Every Coach Notices Defence**

Ask almost any coach what they’re looking for. You’ll hear the same things. Who’s communicating? Who’s sprinting back? Who’s helping teammates? Who’s diving for loose balls? Who’s competing every possession?

Those players earn trust. And trust earns playing time. Parents sometimes focus on how many points their child scored. Coaches often remember everything else.

## **Defence Builds Confidence Too**

There’s another reason defence matters. It gives kids something they can control. You can’t guarantee every shot goes in. You can guarantee effort. You can guarantee communication. You can guarantee hustle.

When kids realise they can still help their team even if they’re having a tough shooting night, something changes. They stop worrying so much. They play freer. Ironically, that often helps their offence as well. Confidence grows when players know they have more than one way to contribute.

## **The Best Players Do Both**

Watch the best young players. Yes, they score. But they also defend. They sprint back after turnovers. They pressure the ball. They communicate. They celebrate great defensive stops just as much as great baskets. That’s why they stay on the court.

Great offence gets attention. Great defence earns trust. The players who master both become the ones coaches can’t afford to take off the floor.
